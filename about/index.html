PWLLHELI BUSINESS IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT

WHAT IS A BID?

BID stands for Business Improvement District.

A BID is a way for local businesses to work together to invest in the town where they trade.

If Pwllheli became a BID, eligible businesses would pay a small levy into a dedicated fund. That money would then be spent on projects and services that aim to make Pwllheli cleaner, safer, more attractive and better for businesses, residents and visitors.

HOW WOULD IT WORK?

A BID is created through a vote by eligible local businesses.

Before a vote takes place, a detailed plan would be produced setting out:

  • what the BID wants to achieve
  • which businesses would pay the levy
  • how much they would pay
  • what the money would be spent on
  • how the BID would be managed and held accountable

If businesses vote in favour, the BID can then operate for a set period, with the levy providing a regular source of funding for the projects in the plan.

HOW MUCH WOULD IT COST?

The amount each business pays depends on the BID's agreed levy rate and the business's circumstances.

The aim is to create a fund that is large enough to make a visible difference, while keeping the cost to individual businesses reasonable.

For Pwllheli, a BID could potentially raise around £100,000 a year to invest in improvements in the town.

The final amount would depend on the businesses included in the BID and the levy rate agreed in the BID proposal.

WHAT COULD THE MONEY PAY FOR?

The money could be used for practical improvements that make a visible difference to Pwllheli.

For example, it could help fund:

  • Better lighting and attractive displays
  • Christmas trees, Pride bunting and other seasonal decorations
  • Flowers, planters and other greenery
  • Extra bin collections during the summer
  • Pedestrianised streets on certain days or for special events
  • Speed cameras or other measures to improve road safety, where permitted
  • Noise cameras or other measures to tackle anti-social driving, where permitted
  • Improvements that make the town centre more attractive to visitors
  • Events, promotions and activities that encourage people to visit and spend time in Pwllheli
  • Other projects identified by local businesses as priorities

The important point is that this is extra money that would be spent locally, on local priorities agreed by business owners.

WHICH OTHER TOWNS HAVE BIDS?

BIDs are already used in towns across Wales and England. Two notable examples are Llangollen and Swansea city centre.

They are particularly useful where local businesses want to work together and have additional funding available to improve their town.

Pwllheli would not be doing something unusual. Many similar market towns have used the BID model to bring businesses together and invest in their local area.

THE IDEA FOR PWLLHELI

The question is simple:

Could a BID give Pwllheli an extra £100,000 or so each year to invest in the town?

It could mean more investment in the things people see and experience every day - cleaner streets, better displays, greener public spaces, seasonal events, improved safety and a town centre that feels more welcoming.

A BID would not replace the work or responsibilities of Cyngor Gwynedd or Pwllheli Town Council. Instead, it would provide "additional" funds for projects and improvements that local business owners want.
